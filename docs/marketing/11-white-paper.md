# Atlas Automation — White Paper

### The next generation of media automation: modular, multi-channel, and built to last

_A non-technical overview for broadcast and media executives. For the engineering detail,
see the [Technical Brief](../01-technical-brief.md)._

---

## Executive summary

Media operations have never been under more pressure. Audiences expect more content across
more platforms — broadcast, streaming, HbbTV, web, and social — while budgets and headcount
stay flat. The tools many broadcasters rely on were built for a single-channel, single-format
world and have grown brittle with age.

**Atlas** is a ground-up rebuild of a proven media automation platform, re-engineered as a
modern, service-based system. One Atlas deployment runs **multiple stations and channels of
different types**, adapts to each team's **workflow**, speaks **multiple languages**, and
connects cleanly to the systems around it. It is designed to be **maintainable, extendable,
secure, and cross-platform** — so it keeps pace with the industry instead of holding you back.

This paper explains the problem Atlas solves, the principles behind its design, and the value
it delivers — without the engineering jargon.

## The problem

Traditional media automation tends to be:

- **Monolithic.** One large system where a change to any part risks the whole. Upgrades are
  rare, painful, and expensive.
- **Single-purpose.** Built for one channel or one content type; running a second station
  means a second system.
- **Rigid.** Workflows are hard-coded. When your process changes, you wait for the vendor.
- **Siloed.** Getting content in and out — to an EPG, a website, a social account, a partner
  — means custom, fragile integrations.
- **Opaque.** When something goes wrong, it's hard to see what happened and why.

The result is a system that resists change in an industry defined by it.

## The Atlas approach

Atlas is built from **independent services**, each responsible for one job — bringing media
in, converting it, describing it, storing it, scheduling it, publishing it. They work together
over a shared messaging backbone, but each can be updated, scaled, or fixed on its own.

That single architectural choice unlocks the platform's advantages:

### One platform, many channels
A single Atlas deployment can operate several stations — a TV channel, a radio station, a news
service — side by side, with each team's content, users, and look-and-feel kept separate.
Grow by adding channels, not systems.

### Your workflow, not ours
Atlas ships with proven process flows and lets your team **adapt them or build new ones** —
for a category, a content type, or a specific use — without waiting on a software release. The
system bends to how you already work.

### Built to scale with demand
When a flood of content arrives, Atlas can **spin up extra processing power automatically** to
clear the backlog, then release it when the rush is over. You pay for capacity when you need
it, not around the clock.

### Content in, content out — cleanly
Atlas makes it straightforward to **import** from external sources and **publish** to the many
destinations modern media requires: electronic program guides, HbbTV, websites, social
platforms, and partner systems — through configurable feeds rather than one-off code.

### Secure and accountable by design
Access is controlled centrally, down to the department and the individual action. **Every
action is logged**, so you always know who did what and when — essential for collaboration,
compliance, and trust.

### Intelligence where it helps
Atlas uses AI to **assist** — detecting faces and content, generating subtitles, suggesting
metadata — always as suggestions your team confirms, never as an unaccountable black box, and
never in a way that blocks the core work if it's unavailable.

## What a day with Atlas looks like

1. **Media arrives** — uploaded, pulled from a watched folder, or recorded from a
   broadcast — and Atlas checks it meets your standards before accepting it.
2. **It's described and prepared** — your team adds the essentials; Atlas generates preview
   and broadcast versions automatically and can suggest metadata with AI.
3. **It moves through your workflow** — review, assignment, and approval, with everyone
   notified in real time and nothing usable on air until it's signed off.
4. **It's scheduled** — placed into the program table for its channel.
5. **It goes to air and beyond** — sent to the control room for playout and published to your
   digital destinations.

Every step is visible, every action is recorded, and the interface each person sees is tuned
to their role and their preferences.

## The bottom line

Atlas replaces a rigid, aging system with a modular platform that **grows with your
organization, adapts to your processes, and connects to your world** — while staying secure,
observable, and cost-effective. It is media automation built for the next decade, by a team
that has already built it once and learned what the next generation needs.
